🌸 Phrase 一穷二白

Imagine you have just moved to a new city with nothing but a suitcase. Your bank account reads zero. Your apartment is bare — no furniture, no dishes, no decorations. Not a single familiar thing surrounds you. That feeling of starting with absolutely nothing is exactly what Chinese speakers reach for when they say 一穷二白 (yī qióng èr bái).

Picture two snapshots side by side. In the first, someone's wallet is completely flat — not a coin inside. In the second, their shelves are completely bare — not a single possession on them. Put those two images together and you have 一穷二白: one, poverty; two, blankness. The phrase stacks two kinds of emptiness to paint a vivid picture of having nothing at all — no money, no resources, no foundation to build on.

The simple rule is this: when Chinese describes a state of total emptiness by pairing a number with a descriptive word, it is amplifying the idea, not doing arithmetic. Here, one and two do not mean "a little poor and a little empty" — they act like bullet points listing everything that is missing, driving home the completeness of the lack.

That blank-slate moment, before anything was built, is the feeling 一穷二白 captures. Can you describe your own "starting from zero" story in one sentence?
