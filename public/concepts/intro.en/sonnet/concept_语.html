Everyday example: Think about the word language itself. When you say someone "speaks the language of kindness," you mean they communicate in a way others understand and feel. The Chinese character 语 (yǔ) carries exactly that spirit — it means language, speech, or words used to communicate.

Picture this: Imagine two friends sitting across from each other. One opens their mouth and sounds come out; the other nods, understanding every word. That invisible bridge between them — built from shared sounds and meanings — is 语 in action. It's not just noise; it's organized, meaningful expression.

Simple rule: 语 refers to language and speech as a system of communication. It appears in words like 语言 (yǔyán, "language"), 汉语 (Hànyǔ, "Chinese language"), and 语法 (yǔfǎ, "grammar"). Wherever humans turn thoughts into words that others can understand, 语 is the concept at work. In the phrase 寡言少语 — describing someone who speaks very little — 语 highlights that even silence is defined against the backdrop of language.
